The market at a glance
1 · Where the work is going
The nonresidential dollars are real, but the growth is not evenly spread.

Total nonresidential construction was roughly flat to down over the last year (about −1.4% in 2025). Underneath that average, one category is running away from the rest: data centers, up 27% year over year, now larger than all other office construction combined. Power is climbing with it. Manufacturing is cooling as the semiconductor and battery mega-projects wind down.

Annualized spending rate (SAAR), latest month. Hover a bar for detail. Growth shown where a clean year-over-year read is public.
What this means for RCA
Chase the roofs that sit on the growth. Data centers, power and the AI-driven build-out are where new large-format nonresidential envelopes are going, while traditional commercial and retail are soft. A roll-up can point its commercial and production capacity at the segments that are actually expanding instead of the market average.
In a sales conversation
"The market's flat on paper, but that hides a 27% data-center surge. We're positioning crews and capacity where the envelope work is growing, not where the headline is."

What this means for RCA
The large-format envelope work is concentrated where the data centers are going: Texas, Virginia, Georgia, Arizona and the Sun Belt. RCA is headquartered in Atlanta, in the number-three state, with companies across the Sun Belt, exactly where the wave is landing. That is a footprint advantage worth pressing.
In a sales conversation
"We're already on the ground where the build-out is heaviest. When the envelope work comes, we're not chasing it into a new market, we're standing in it."
2 · The two signals that disagree
Architects are billing less. Owners are planning more.

The two best forward reads on nonresidential work are pointing opposite directions right now, and the gap is the story. Architecture billings, which lead construction by roughly nine to twelve months, have been below the growth line for 41 straight months. Dodge's planning momentum, which tracks projects entering the pipeline, is surging on data-center and institutional work. Weak near-term design, strong long-horizon planning.

What this means for RCA
Plan capacity for a soft next few quarters and a busier back half of the horizon. The billings weakness says do not over-hire into the near term; the planning surge says the data-center and institutional wave is coming, so lock crews, materials and acquisition targets before it lands and pricing tightens.
In a sales conversation
"Design work is quiet today, but planning is up 51% over the year. The owners building the next wave are choosing partners now. That's the window."
3 · Cost and financing backdrop
Input inflation is cooler than the peak, but labor is still the lever.

Roofing is labor-intensive, so the biggest controllable margin risk is labor, not shingles. Contractor prices are still rising faster than the broad material basket, and financing costs have eased from their highs into the mid-six-percent range, a modest tailwind for owner demand.

What this means for RCA
Write escalation into bids on the labor line, not just materials. With contractor-price inflation running ahead of the material basket, the defensible price story is labor, and it holds up in an owner conversation because the public index backs it.
